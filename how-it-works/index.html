How Screen Walk works

Screen Walk keeps blocking the apps you choose. Walking tops up minutes on your card, and you start a break of 5 to 60 minutes when you want your apps.

Choose what to block

You pick the apps and websites that pull you in with Apple’s Screen Time picker. The picker hands Screen Walk opaque tokens, so the app cannot see which apps you chose, and the choice stays on your phone. Screen Walk then keeps blocking them with Apple’s Screen Time technology.

Walking tops up minutes

Steps come from Apple Health: a phone in a pocket or an Apple Watch. By default 250 steps top up 1 minute, and you can set the pace from 100 to 1,000 steps a minute. Today shows your card, how many steps are left until the next minute, and a short log of your walks and breaks.

Start a break

On Today, tap Start a break and choose how many minutes, from 5 to 60. Then press and hold your card on the reader. Your chosen apps open for that long and close again on their own, even if Screen Walk is closed. If you end a break early, the minutes you did not use come back to the card.

When the card is low

An emergency break opens your apps for 5 minutes and uses no minutes. You get one a day by default; Settings lets you choose 0 to 3. Today offers it when the card holds fewer than 5 minutes.

Minutes you don’t use

Minutes you don’t use carry over into tomorrow, then drop off the card. Breaks use the oldest minutes first.

Turning blocking off is deliberate

In Settings you press and hold Turn off blocking for 15 seconds and confirm. Blocking then turns off at a coming midnight, at least 12 hours after you confirm, so the wait is 12 to 36 hours. A pop-up names the real date, and Keep blocking on cancels it until then. Turning blocking back on is never delayed. The wait is there so that a bad moment does not decide it.

Blocking hours

Blocking can run all the time or only on the days and hours you choose. Read how blocking hours work.

Today, Insights and goals

Today is the card, one sentence about the day and the short log. Insights gives a plain verdict on your week with a simple chart. Step and calorie goals are optional and never change how many minutes you get.

What it does not do

  • It is for your own use. It is not a parental control app.
  • It does not count anything but steps, and steps typed in by hand are ignored.
  • It has no streaks and does not nag you.
  • It cannot see which apps you chose.
  • The interface is English only.

The price is 7 free days, then one payment, with no subscription. See the price page.

Questions

Does Screen Walk work with an Apple Watch?

Yes. Steps come from Apple Health, so steps your Apple Watch records count, as do steps from your iPhone.

Can I top up minutes any way other than walking?

No. Walking is the only way minutes are topped up. Steps entered by hand in the Health app are ignored.

What if I close Screen Walk during a break?

Blocking still comes back when the break ends. It does not depend on the app being open.
